# ADR-002 Activity ingest via an on-track iOS app reading Apple Health

Status: **proposed** - depends on ADR-001's blocking question
Date: 2026-09-25
Owner: architect

## Context

The customer states on-track wants an iPhone app regardless, for offline
shopping lists, sync, and on-device barcode scanning. That changes option D in
ADR-001 from "build a product to get a feature" into "add a feature to a product
already being built", and removes the objection the product owner raised in
`scope.md`.

## The chain

```
watch -> Garmin Connect app -> Apple Health -> on-track iOS app -> on-track API
```

Four hops. Garmin Connect must be installed and its Apple Health connection
enabled by the user; each hop fails silently.

## What it involves

**Entitlement and permission.** HealthKit entitlement, an
`NSHealthShareUsageDescription` string, and per-type read authorisation the user
grants explicitly. A privacy policy is mandatory for App Store review.

**Waking on a new activity.** `enableBackgroundDelivery` for the workout type,
plus an `HKObserverQuery`. HealthKit wakes the app when samples are saved, at
most once per the frequency requested. Observer queries say *that* something
changed, not *what*, so the wake handler must then query.

**Finding what is new.** `HKAnchoredObjectQuery` returns only what has changed
since the previous anchor. This is the iOS-side answer to the delivery-state
spike in `scope.md` - the anchor is the "what have we already sent" ledger, and
it is maintained by HealthKit rather than by us.

**Route data.** `HKWorkoutRouteQuery` retrieves the GPS track. Known caveat: on
a background wake the workout arrives before its route is populated, so the
route needs a deferred second fetch rather than being read inline.

**Reliability.** Background delivery is best-effort and iOS may defer it, so the
app also needs a catch-up sync when opened in the foreground. The delivery
ledger still matters; HealthKit's anchor covers the Health-to-app hop only, not
app-to-on-track.

## Benefits beyond the sync itself

**The personal access token problem largely disappears** for iOS users. The app
holds an ordinary authenticated session, so there is no step where an athlete
generates and pastes a token. That is most of EPIC-003's concern resolved as a
side effect.

**Vendor coverage.** Anything that writes to Apple Health arrives - Coros,
Polar, Apple Watch - which answers Q8 without further work. EPIC-004 shrinks to
Android.

## Costs

Apple Developer Program about GBP 80/yr, incurred anyway once on-track ships an
app. Cloud Run ingest at this volume is effectively free and scales to zero.
Marginal cost per user tends to zero, satisfying the customer's requirement that
cost falls with scale.

## What it does not give us

on-track's stated analysis inputs are splits, heart rate, distance, elevation
and ground contact time. Measured against what Garmin Connect actually writes to
Apple Health:

| Input | Available via Apple Health |
|---|---|
| Heart rate | yes, as samples |
| Distance | yes, as a workout total |
| Elevation | probably, as workout summary metadata - unconfirmed |
| **Splits** | **no** - raw FIT lap-by-lap telemetry does not sync |
| **Ground contact time** | **no** - running dynamics do not sync |

Garmin syncs steps, distance, active energy, heart rate, sleep and workout
summaries. Running dynamics (cadence, ground contact time, vertical
oscillation), cycling power, advanced swim metrics and lap-by-lap telemetry all
stay in Garmin's ecosystem.

The customer has accepted losing ground contact time as a degradation rather
than a blocker. Splits are a larger loss and were named first.

**And the two recommendations collide.** Splits could only be reconstructed by
taking the GPS route and deriving them from it - the very data ADR-002
recommends declining on privacy grounds. So the choice is:

- decline routes, accept that run analysis reduces to a summary plus heart rate
- take routes, reconstruct splits, and accept the largest new privacy exposure
  and a wider DPIA

That is a product and privacy trade, not an architectural one. Product owner.

No historic backfill from Garmin into Apple Health either.

## Data protection

**The duty already exists.** on-track is a diet and exercise planner, so it
already processes health data - special category data under UK GDPR Article 9,
requiring an Article 9 condition on top of a lawful basis, realistically
explicit consent for a commercial product. This feature widens the scope of
existing obligations; it does not create a new category of them.

**What genuinely is new:**

1. **Location data.** Workout routes reveal where someone runs, and start and
   end points reveal where they live. This is the single largest new risk and
   the reason to apply data minimisation hard - see the recommendation below.
2. **Apple's contractual terms, separate from law.** App Store Review Guideline
   5.1.3 forbids using or disclosing health and fitness data for advertising,
   marketing or data mining, and forbids storing personal health information in
   iCloud. Enforced by App Review, and a common rejection cause. Storing it on
   on-track's own backend for the purpose of providing the service is the
   accepted pattern; sharing it onward is not.
3. **A DPIA becomes clearly necessary** rather than arguable. ICO guidance
   points to a DPIA for health data processing, new technologies processing
   special category data, and systematic monitoring - this hits all three.
4. **App Store privacy labels** must accurately declare health and location
   collection and linkage to identity.

**Already true and unchanged:** the ICO data protection fee under the Data
Protection (Charges and Information) Regulations 2018 (tiered; confirm the
current amount), a processor agreement with Google for GCP, retention and
deletion policy, and subject access and erasure handling.

**One actionable point:** GCP region must be UK or EU to avoid an international
transfer problem. Worth confirming where on-track's Cloud Run service and its
data actually sit.

## Recommendation

**Do not ingest workout routes in the first version.** A diet and exercise
planner needs duration, distance, pace, heart rate and activity type to reconcile
against a plan. It does not need to know the streets someone ran down. Declining
the route data removes the largest new privacy risk, shrinks the DPIA's scope,
avoids the route-availability race on background wake, and costs nothing in
product value that anyone has articulated.

If routes are wanted later, add them deliberately, with their own consent and
their own entry in the DPIA.
